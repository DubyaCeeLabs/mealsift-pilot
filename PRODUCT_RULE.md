# MealSift product rule

## Mission
**Better than 5 minutes ago.**

## Definitive product-development rule
For every product build, including MealSift:

1. Identify the strongest current market performer using available evidence. Define the comparison dimension (for example, adoption, ratings, retention, or revenue); do not present public rankings as definitive when they are not disclosed.
2. Investigate why it performs: audience, core job, workflow, features, pricing, distribution, and customer feedback.
3. Use proven strengths as a guide for an original product. Do not clone protected code, content, branding, or proprietary assets.
4. Improve documented weaknesses or friction with specific design and product changes.
5. Validate the improvement with observable pilot measures before claiming superiority. Keep claims proportional to evidence.
6. Repeat the review as the market and customer evidence change.

## MealSift application
The evidence reviewed for this pilot suggests Samsung Food is a reach and breadth benchmark, while AnyList is a high-rating/satisfaction benchmark. Public install and rating figures are imperfect proxies and do not establish revenue or retention leadership. Their proven core loop is recipe discovery or saving → weekly meal planning → consolidated and shareable grocery lists.

MealSift should deliver that loop with clear, dependable execution: complete recipe instructions, serving-scaled quantities, transparent ingredient sources, editable custom groceries, and explicit “I already have this” checks. A checked item remains visible; the app must not silently omit pantry items. Avoid making claims of market superiority until customer tests support them.

## Current pilot boundaries
- Original recipe text and assets only.
- Browser-local plan and preferences; no account, cloud sync, tracking, checkout, or payment collection.
- Build for low-friction use first. Add recurring paid access only after a distinct ongoing benefit and successful payment/access QA exist.
- Household sync, retailer integration, large-scale recipe imports, and AI features require evidence, safety review where relevant, and approved operating costs.
